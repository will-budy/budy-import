    Platforms and platform sessions¶
    Document Platforms, platform sessions, the platform claim and system audiences
    Status Design agreed with the owner on 2026-11-20; implementation in progress (see "Rollout")
    Audience Platform engineers changing STS, the portal, a cluster gateway or OpenFGA platform policy
    Record de-v2 gitops docs/spikes/2026-11-19-platform-and-audience-claims.md (the audit behind this page)

    A platform groups tenants for management. Only platform staff, who hold a platform role and assume into it, use one. Customers and digital teammates work in tenant sessions and never see a platform. This page says:

    • which platforms exist;
    • how a platform session is issued and what its token carries;
    • how a path declares that it needs a platform or a system audience;
    • how a new platform is added so that it is managed exactly like an existing one.

    Platforms that exist¶

    Platform What it is Tenants under it Who uses it
    budy-sl-platform-1 Business platform: senior living customer tenants, and the service tenants dt-manager-1, dt-consulting-services-1, karkhana-cluster-1, pitcrew-cluster-1, communication-cluster-1 staff, provisioning and reviewer services
    budy-general-platform-1 Business platform: general (new) new customer tenants the same staff and services as budy-sl-platform-1
    budy Auth Manager's own platform: role administration across platforms (role_management_platform) and a few service grants none Auth Manager administrators
    system-platform-1 Context operator's platform none context-operator
    janus-platform-1 Janus workloads' platform (viewer only) none janus-intake-1, janus-outbound-policy-1

    The two business platforms are parallel and configured identically (see "Adding a business platform"). The other three are infrastructure platforms; this page does not change them.

    Two meanings of "audience"¶

    OAuth audience System audience
    Claim aud system_audience_key
    Value the receiving service: urn:budy:nexus:<endpoint>:<Service>, an app origin, or authz-manager-1 an OpenFGA audience: global, seniorliving, global-internal, global-early, authz-manager
    Set by the portal route (resource) the person, in system scope, when the route needs one
    Required always, by every receiver: it stops a token for one service being replayed at another only by paths that act on system-scope Context content

    In the rest of this page, "audience" without a qualifier means the system audience.

    An audience is a grouping of tenants, and it has two separate edges:

    • Members (audience#linked_tenant): the tenants in it, set per tenant from the tenant's audiences input. A tenant may be on either platform, so an audience can span both: global holds tenants of both business platforms, while seniorliving holds only the senior-living tenants linked to it. Reading an audience's content (can_view_audience_context) follows membership.
    • Manager (audience#managed_by_platform): exactly one platform, which administers membership (can_manage_audience_membership) and authors and publishes the audience's system-scope Context content (context_scope#system_platform). The STS refuses a system-scope session for an audience with no managing platform or more than one.

    Today all five Context audiences are managed by budy-sl-platform-1, including global, whose members span both business platforms. A system-scope Context session for global is therefore opened on budy-sl-platform-1; staff hold the same roles on both platforms, so nothing is lost. budy-general-platform-1 manages no audience until one of its own is created.

    The session token¶

    Claim names are the standard snake_case names (formerly camelCase: scopeType, scopeId, sessionBoundary, glassboxId, systemAudienceKey). See STS token claims.

    Claim Tenant session Platform session
    scope_type tenant platform
    scope_id the tenant the platform
    tenant the tenant absent
    platform absent the platform, equal to scope_id
    system_audience_key absent present only when the route needed an audience and one was chosen
    session_boundary viewer, editor or admin viewer, editor or admin
    glassbox_id the billing entity absent
    aud the receiving service the receiving service

    Rules every validator applies:

    • A token with both tenant and platform is malformed.
    • A token whose platform differs from scope_id is malformed.
    • A platform token without a platform is malformed.
    • system_audience_key on a tenant token is malformed.
    • platform and system_audience_key are reserved: an exchange policy's receiver claims cannot set them. Only the STS sets them, from a decision it checked in OpenFGA.

    During the rollout, a validator that finds no platform on a platform token reads scope_id. The STS issues one-hour sessions, so this fallback is removed one release after every STS is issuing the claim.

    Workload platform sessions, such as Janus intake-1 on janus-platform-1 and dt-provisioner-1, carry platform like human ones. The rule is "every platform-scope token", not "every human".

    Issuing a platform session¶

    The portal sends a pushed authorization request with:

    • budy_scope_type=platform
    • budy_scope_id=<platform>
    • budy_system_audience_key=<audience>, only when the route needs an audience.

    Before it issues a code, the STS checks:

    1. The person may assume the platform at the chosen boundary: assumable_platform_<boundary> on platform:<id>, from ResolveSessionScope. A platform role needs internal_user on the same platform; Auth Manager writes that marker with each platform membership.
    2. The resource admits the platform: Nexus can_call through operation#allowed_platform.
    3. If an audience was sent, the platform manages it: managed_by_platform on audience:<key>. The browser's pair is never trusted.

    The STS repeats these checks at authorize, at code redemption and at handoff. With no audience, step 3 is skipped and the token carries no system_audience_key; whether that is acceptable is the receiver's decision, not the STS's.

    POST /v2/system-session/query answers the portal:

    • With no platformId: {platforms: [{id, sessionBoundaries}]}, the platforms the person may assume for the resource. This is what fills the platform dropdown.
    • With a platformId and no audienceKey: {sessionBoundaries} for that platform.
    • With both: as before, the boundaries for that platform and audience.

    The portal: per-route needs¶

    A route says what a launch in system scope needs. The flags sit in the route's session block in the platform-ui values, next to the session boundaries:

    context:
  nexusTarget:
    temporalNexusEndpoint: ob1-nexus-context-cluster-1-endpoint-1
    nexusService: ContextServiceV2
    platformSession:
      sessionBoundaries: [viewer, editor, admin]
      needsAudience: true      # system-scope Context acts on one audience's content
janus-console-1:
  nexusTarget:
    platformSession:
      sessionBoundaries: [viewer, editor, admin]
      needsAudience: false     # Janus's system views are platform-wide
    • The platform is always picked in system scope. The dropdown lists only the platforms the STS says the person can assume for that app, at any boundary. With one platform it is preselected. There is no flag for it: a platform session without a platform cannot exist.
    • An audience is picked only when needsAudience: true. The audience dropdown then lists the audiences of the chosen platform, from the portal configuration platforms: [{id, label, audiences: [{key, label}]}]. It replaces the flat systemAudiences list, whose entries carried their platform.
    • Tenant scope is unchanged. Pick a tenant and a user; no platform and no audience.
    • The server re-checks both on launch; the browser's choice is a request, not a fact.
    • A route without needsAudience keeps today's behaviour, which requires an audience, until its values say otherwise.
    • Auth Manager is not affected: it always launches on budy with the authz-manager audience (authzControlPlane.platformAudience).

    Gateways and components¶

    The shared Go module go/capability gives a gateway the token's platform:

    • stsauth.Identity.Platform;
    • dispatch.Command.Platform and dispatch.ComponentRequest.Platform;
    • {platform} in a capability's authorize.object.
    upsertTenant:
  authorize:
    relation: may_administer_gitjib_1
    object: platform:{platform}
    • {platform} with a tenant token renders nothing, and the call is PERMISSION_DENIED, as {tenant} is with a platform token. A path that needs a platform therefore rejects a token without one.
    • A capability for platform sessions names its object platform:{platform}, never a literal business platform, so one manifest is correct on every business platform. A literal platform id stays legal for a fixed grant checked with a tenant session: events and nainai check platform:budy#may_register_triggers and #may_view_worker_status that way.
    • go/authz GatewayAuthorizer states the live session on the token's platform. It denies, without asking OpenFGA, a platform-scope check on an object naming another platform. So a platform session on one platform never satisfies a check on another, even when the person holds a role on both.

    A component that checks in its activity reads ComponentRequest.Platform; it never takes a platform from a payload.

    A component that works across one platform's tenants keeps a staff session to that platform. go/authz gives it:

    • One tenant: check tenant:<id>#in_platform_session with the caller's session on its own platform (CheckRequest.WithPlatformSession). It holds only for tenants that platform manages.
    • A list: authz.PlatformTenants(ctx, checker, platform, user, boundary) returns that platform's tenants. A truncated answer is a partial view, never the whole platform.

    GitJib admin-1 is the reference. A call that names a tenant is refused unless the tenant is on the caller's platform. Tenant lists, the all-tenant drift audit and the drift report show only that platform's tenants. Settings shared by every tenant (the public package allow-list, the admin-token check) are open to a GitJib admin of either platform. Tenant users are not affected: they reach GitLab through token-broker-1 with a tenant session. The TypeScript console-kit exposes the same as principal.platform. Receivers built on auth-service shared/contract (nexusauth) read VerifiedNexusSessionV1.Platform.

    Adding a business platform¶

    A new business platform is managed by everyone who manages budy-sl-platform-1, with the same roles. No model change is needed: the platform is entities and tuples in authz-config_gitops_repo-1, shared by every environment.

    1. Declare it. In entities/services/entities.json tenants, add {"id": "<platform>", "type": "platform", "version": "v2"}. Do not edit entities/profiles/*/authz-bootstrap.json: it is immutable after first apply.
    2. Role administration. In entities/services/service-bootstrap.yaml, add platform:budy role_management_platform platform:<platform>. Auth Manager administrators can then grant roles on it.
    3. Service admissions. Copy every allowed_platform tuple of budy-sl-platform-1:
    4. service-bootstrap.yaml, shared-policy.yaml;
    5. the dev-only entities/profiles/dev/profile-provisioning.yaml.

    Also copy may_write_directories for the provisioner. 4. Service roles. In entities.json memberships, copy each service user's roles on budy-sl-platform-1: - dt-provisioner-1: platform-admin, tenant-provisioner, system-context-author; - dt-reviewer-1: system-context-reviewer; - backend-1: tenant-provisioner.

    Auth Manager writes a service user's internal_user marker on every platform where it holds a platform role, so these roles take effect. Until 2026-11 the marker was written on the service's home platform only. 5. Staff roles. Two ways, by how much the role can do: - platform-admin: GitOps. authz-config entities/profiles/<env>/staff-platform-roles.json lists each environment's staff admins per platform (today Jae and Samay on both business platforms). Add the new platform's lines there. The file sets releaseRemovedMemberships, so removing a line revokes the role. It has no UI assigners on purpose. - platform-maintainer, platform-support, platform-viewer: the Auth Manager UI. Open Roles and pick the platform as the target; a second authz admin approves, and nobody can assign themselves. The API equivalent is PUT /v1/tenants/<platform>/members/<user>/roles/<role>.

    Auth Manager writes each person's internal_user marker on the platform with the membership. In GitJib, platform-admin opens the admin console (may_administer_gitjib_1, admin session); platform-maintainer manages the public package allow-list (may_administer_gitjib_packages_1, editor session); platform-viewer sees the read-only views. In Janus, platform-maintainer and above manage connection types (may_administer_janus_connection_types_1, editor session). 6. Audiences. Membership: a customer created without audiences gets its platform's default from the customer tenant type (budy-platform-crs customer@v7: global and seniorliving under senior living, global only under general). Platform records cannot change after creation, so the default lives in the type: a further platform needs a new customer version. Management, optional: an audience managed by the new platform, for Context system scope there, is platform:<platform> managed_by_platform audience:<key>, plus the portal's platforms[].audiences. An existing audience keeps its one manager. 7. Portal. Add the platform's label (and audiences) to the platform-ui platforms list.

    models/business-platforms-test.store.fga.yaml checks:

    • the two business platforms grant the same things;
    • a session on one authorizes nothing on the other;
    • a role on the other does not help.

    Tenants under a platform¶

    A tenant belongs to the platform at the top of its parent chain: Auth Manager writes managed_by_platform from it, and creating the tenant needs can_create_tenants on that platform.

    An Auth Manager administrator can create a customer tenant under either platform in Auth Manager by choosing the platform as its parent. dt-provisioner-1 (provisionTenantProfileV1) creates customer tenants under budy-sl-platform-1; creating them under the caller's platform is a dt-manager change that is not released, and dt-provisioner-1 is due to move to nainai.

    A customer created without audiences joins its platform's default audiences (customer@v7): global and seniorliving under senior living, global only under general. Audiences given at creation are used as given. Auth Manager stores the resolved list on the tenant, so it does not change if a later version defaults differently.

    Rollout¶

    Claim names in this table are the ones at the time of each step; they are now the snake_case names (scope_id, ...).

    Step Repository Change Backward compatible because
    1 authz-config declare and mirror budy-general-platform-1 inert until roles are granted
    2 common-modules platform in Go and TS, {platform}, authorizer fix falls back to scopeId
    3 auth-service STS platform claim, optional audience, platform listing; nexusauth; Auth Manager service marker an audience is still accepted and checked
    4 dt-manager tenant parent from the caller's platform (optional; dt-provisioner-1 moves to nainai) budy-sl-platform-1 callers see no change
    5 budy-platform-crs customer@v7: default audiences per platform existing tenants keep their stored audiences

    8 de-v2 gitops route flags, platforms list, chart and library versions per environment, dev first
    9 Auth Manager (data) grant staff roles on budy-general-platform-1 —
    10 common-modules, auth-service remove the scopeId fallback one release after step 3 everywhere
