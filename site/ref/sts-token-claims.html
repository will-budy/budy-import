    STS token claims¶
    Document The claims the STS puts in its tokens, the standard-claims migration, and the rules for reading and adding claims
    Status Standard claims issued since 2026-11-21; the deprecated claims stopped being minted in sts-service 1.18.0 (dev 2026-11-22); stage and prod are owner-managed (see "Rollout")
    Audience Anyone who writes code that reads an STS token, asks the STS for one, or wants a new claim
    Source auth-service-1: docs/v2-token-claims-contract.md (the claims registry there is the source of truth)

    The STS (auth-cluster-1, sts-service) mints RS256 JWTs: tenant and platform sessions, session-exchange tokens, actor tokens, external app tokens, run tokens, connection sessions and registration tokens. This page says what they carry now, why that changed, and the rules that keep it from drifting again.

    Why the claims changed¶

    Until November 2026 the STS expressed what a token may do in one place that only Nexus understood: nexusAccess (aud, operations, capabilities). Next to it sat camelCase claims that existed only because Nexus gateways read them (scopeType, sessionBoundary, glassboxId, and others) and a scope claim that held an OAuth-style label, not a standard scope. Only the auth service minted them, and every cluster hand-read them.

    The result: HTTP receivers and Nexus receivers needed different token handling, no standard library could validate a token, and every new fact (an environment, a call context) meant a new Nexus-shaped claim that each cluster had to learn.

    The standardization drive moved the STS to one claim set that serves HTTP and Nexus receivers alike. That is the platform's design direction: no separate external and internal token shapes, and the Nexus practice evolves into the HTTP one (see the migration guide, section 1).

    Standard and custom claims¶

    Between 2026-11-21 and the removal release (sts-service 1.18.0, dev 2026-11-22) the STS minted each deprecated claim next to its replacement with a byte-equal value. Since 1.18.0 it mints only the standard names; the "Deprecated" rows below are what tokens minted before the release carried, and what the reader code still accepts (see "Deprecation").

    Claim Kind Meaning
    iss, sub, aud, exp, iat, jti standard (RFC 7519) Unchanged. aud is always a single string.
    nbf standard Issued at minus 30 seconds on session, exchange, actor, app and run tokens.
    scope standard (RFC 8693, RFC 9068) Space-delimited, audience-qualified. See "The scope claim".
    client_id standard (RFC 8693) The OAuth client the token was issued to. On a run token, the attesting caller.
    act.sub standard (RFC 8693) The actor of a delegation. Unchanged.
    tenant, platform, dt, automation, workflow, activity registered Unchanged.
    scope_type registered, snake_case tenant or platform. Formerly scopeType.
    scope_id registered The tenant or platform id. Formerly scopeId.
    session_boundary registered viewer, editor or admin. Formerly sessionBoundary.
    glassbox_id registered The billing entity, tenant sessions only. Formerly glassboxId.
    system_audience_key registered Platform sessions, when a system audience was chosen. Formerly systemAudienceKey.
    kubernetes_namespace registered Tenant sessions, when the resource needs one. Formerly kubernetesNamespace.
    workload_identity registered Kubernetes self-exchange. Formerly workloadIdentity.
    token_class registered One name for what was sessionClass (sessions), tokenClass (run tokens) and token_use (actor tokens).
    run_id, attested_by registered Run tokens. Formerly runId, attestedBy.
    call{} registered The context of one call through a connection: connection, publication, activity, attested_by. Formerly act.conn, act.pub, act.activity, act.attestedBy.
    env registered dev, stage, ...; absent in prod. See "The env claim".
    dep_scope deprecated, still minted The old scope label (session:editor, app:read, signup, delegation:read) on exchange, actor and app tokens; the only carrier of that legacy value. The scope key holds the standard scope. It is removed in a separate, later release.
    nexusAccess (aud, operations, capabilities) removed in 1.18.0 Replaced by aud and the scope entries below.
    act.conn, act.pub, act.activity, act.attestedBy removed in 1.18.0 Replaced by call{}. act.sub stays.

    The camelCase names (scopeType, scopeId, sessionBoundary, glassboxId, systemAudienceKey, kubernetesNamespace, workloadIdentity, sessionClass, tokenClass, token_use, runId, attestedBy) were removed in 1.18.0 as well. Every claim is in the registry (shared/contract/claims.go) with its status, replacement and dates, and the claims contract renders that registry as a table.

    The scope claim¶

    scope is a space-delimited list of entries, each qualified with the audience of the receiver it is for. For a receiver whose audience is A:

    Entry Grants
    A the root: every capability of A (it does not grant a Nexus operation)
    A/<component> one component
    A/<component>.<capability>[.<sub>] one capability, up to six levels
    A#<Operation> one Nexus operation

    Rules every receiver applies:

    • A grant allows capability P if the entry is the root, or equals P, or P begins with the entry followed by a dot.
    • A receiver that finds no entry for its own audience falls back to the legacy check during the migration; entries for other audiences, and legacy labels, are ignored. Once any entry names the audience, the scope decides, and an operation needs its A#<Operation> entry.
    • Names with a dot inside a segment, or empty names, are refused. Anything unparsable fails closed.
    • Limits: 8192 bytes and 64 entries.
    • The audience is fully qualified (for example urn:budy:nexus:<endpoint>:<Service>, or an app origin) and not environment-qualified. Environment-qualified audiences would multiply the GitOps values; env carries the environment.

    Narrowing (RFC 8693). A caller of the session exchange, the workload exchange or the user-delegation grant may add <resource>/<component>.<capability> entries to the scope it sends, to ask for less. The token then carries those capabilities and no root entry. A request can only narrow within what is already admitted (anything outside is 400 invalid_scope), and a narrowed session cannot be exchanged again or handed off.

    The env claim¶

    env lets a receiver refuse a token from another environment without environment-qualified audiences.

    • The STS stamps env from STS_ENV_CLAIM (chart value envClaim). It is never prod: production stamps none, and the chart refuses prod and malformed names.
    • Receivers choose what they accept with STS_ENV_POLICY:
    Policy Meaning Use
    unchecked (default) env is ignored until the STS stamps env
    absent a token that carries any env is refused prod
    equal:<env> the token's env must equal it; a token without one is refused dev equal:dev, stage equal:stage

    Set equal only after the STS of that environment stamps env and every token minted before has expired.

    Deprecation¶

    • Each claim has a status in the registry: active, deprecated or removed, with its replacement, the date it was deprecated, and remove_not_before.
    • remove_not_before was 2026-11-22 for every deprecated claim. That date was a floor, not a trigger: reaching it only warned. The removal shipped in an owner-confirmed release, sts-service 1.18.0 / contract 1.12.0 (auth chart 0.1.115), promoted to dev on 2026-11-22. It marks every deprecated claim removed except dep_scope, which stays minted and deprecated because the readers of actor, app and sign-up tokens still need it; removing it is a separate, later release.
    • The reader structs in contract (NexusSessionV1, ActorClaimV1, NexusAccessV1) keep the removed names, so a receiver still reads a token that was minted before the removal until it expires. A receiver that reads only a removed name sees an empty claim on a token minted after it and refuses the token.
    • The STS serves the notices at /.well-known/claim-notices.json on its internal profile only (the external profile serves neither the notices nor the Link: rel="deprecation" header that advertises them). The shared gateway module (go/capability, gateway/stsauth) discovers them from the JWKS response and logs them: info for a planned deprecation, warn once the replacement is issued, error once a claim is removed. STS_CLAIM_NOTICES=off switches it off.
    • CI guards: tests make sure that only claims marked deprecated or removed in the registry are, that every one names a replacement, and that no test depends on the calendar date.

    Strict mode¶

    A receiver that is sure every token it sees comes from an STS that issues the standard claims turns on STS_REQUIRE_STANDARD_CLAIMS=true (consoles: CONSOLE_REQUIRE_STANDARD_CLAIMS). Then scope must have an entry for its audience, the snake_case twin of every deprecated claim the validator reads must be present, nexusAccess is never consulted, a Nexus operation must be named, and the audience must be one string. Only the literal true (any case) turns it on.

    Size limits¶

    Tokens that travel in a cookie must stay under 2700 bytes; the STS warns above 2400. Removing the deprecated claims made tokens about a quarter smaller: a tenant session for the Context audience went from 2169 to 1574 bytes, and a session exchange for the longest audience from 2700 to 2034 bytes. The limits are unchanged. Do not add claims lightly.

    Rules going forward¶

    1. Read standard claims. Use scope, client_id, nbf, env and the registered snake_case claims. Do not read a camelCase claim or nexusAccess.
    2. Use the shared validators, not hand-rolled parsing: go/capability/gateway/stsauth for gateways and HTTP receivers, shared/contract/nexusauth for Nexus receivers, @budybot/console-kit for consoles. They implement the scope grammar, the twin rules and the notices.
    3. No Nexus-specific claims. HTTP and Nexus receivers read the same claims; there is no separate Nexus claim or token shape. A Nexus operation is the operation entry <aud>#<Operation> of the scope grammar (an HTTP receiver can ignore it), and a capability is <aud>/<component>.<capability>.
    4. Do not expand the claim set. A new claim needs the owner's approval, an entry in the registry (name, class, who mints it, who reads it) and a golden test. Prefer a standard claim over a new one, and prefer a scope entry over a new claim for anything that is a permission. Do not put data in a token that a receiver can look up.
    5. Never trust a pair that disagrees. A token minted before the removal could carry a deprecated claim and its twin. If they differ, the token did not come from the STS; refuse it, and name the claims in the error, not the values.

    Rollout¶

    • Dev-1: the STS removal release (1.18.0, chart 0.1.115) reached dev on 2026-11-22. Dev also has the env claim (auth-cluster-1) and, in the context and llm-router gateways (llm-router gateway, request-handler-1 and stream-handler-1), strict mode with STS_ENV_POLICY=equal:dev; Janus gateway-1 and console-1 run strict mode as well. The de-v2 changes are #3263 and #3266.
    • Stage and prod: the owner promotes auth-cluster-1, context and llm-router through Kargo, and I have not checked what has reached them. Every receiver must read the standard names before the removal release reaches its environment. The intended env settings there are equal:stage for stage and absent for prod (and no envClaim in prod); they are not set yet.
    • Other clusters must read the standard names before the release reaches their environment. The per-cluster checklist is in the migration guide: Migrating to the standard STS claims.
